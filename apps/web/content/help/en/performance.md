Performance comes with the Business plan and up. Everyone in the workspace finds it under **Performance**: their goals, their reviews and what waits for them. HR (the workspace admin, HR admins and HR officers) runs the review cycles. Managers see their team: their direct reports and the people below them.

## Goals

A goal belongs to a person, or to a unit for the goals people align theirs with. It has a title, a due date and a weight, and it is one of:

- **an objective**, with key results that are ticked off as they are done;
- **a KPI**, with a measure, a target and whether higher or lower is better.

Some KPIs read their value from Good People instead of being typed: **the attendance rate** from processed attendance, and **output against target** from daily production (the RMG pack). They are refreshed every day, and nobody can type a better number.

- A person drafts their goals for a review cycle, or their manager does. **Submit for agreement** sends them to the manager, who agrees them.
- A person's agreed goals in a cycle carry weights that add up to 100%. Changing an agreed goal's title, measure, target or weight sends it back for agreement.
- **Check-ins** record progress and a note, from the person or the manager, through the period.

## Review cycles

HR creates a cycle under **Performance** → **Review cycles**: its name and period, who is in it (companies, and people who joined on or before a date), whether there are self reviews and peer feedback, the competencies rated, and a date for each step. New cycles start from the competencies and ratings guide in **Performance settings**.

**Launch** creates a review for each person, with the manager they have that day. A later change of manager does not move the review.

Ratings run from 1 to 5: well below expectations, below expectations, meets expectations, above expectations, and well above expectations.

People are reminded three days before each step's date and on the day.

## Self, peer and manager reviews

- **Self review:** the person rates each agreed goal and competency and comments, then submits. Workers without a login skip this step: their manager reviews them alone, so a factory can review its lines without putting every worker in front of a screen.
- **Peer feedback:** the person or their manager names peers. The manager approves the person's choices. Peers answer two questions and give a rating. The person sees peer feedback only after release, only together, and only when at least three peers answered. Only the manager and HR see who wrote what.
- **Manager review:** the manager finds the team's reviews under **My team's reviews**. They see the self review, rate each goal and competency, and propose an overall rating with recommendations: an increment, a promotion to a designation, or an improvement plan. The goal ratings are averaged by the goals' weights.

## Calibration and release

In **Calibration**, HR compares a unit's proposed ratings side by side, next to how the workspace's ratings guide says ratings usually spread. The guide is never a quota: a unit of strong performers can be rated as such.

- HR settles each final rating. A change from the proposed rating needs a reason, kept with who made it.
- **Release the reviews** needs a final rating for every review. Each person is told, reads their own review and acknowledges it, saying whether they agree, with a comment.
- A released review never changes. A disagreement stays on record with it, and the next cycle corrects course.
- The cycle's page has the outcomes (final ratings and recommendations) as a CSV file. They are what the increment round uses.

## Improvement plans

A manager or HR starts an improvement plan for one person under **Improvement plans**: why, from and to (30, 60 or 90 days), what must improve and how it is measured, and the support given. The person reads it and confirms they have read it. Check-ins record how it goes, and the plan closes as successful or not met, or is extended.

A plan records concerns and the help given. Closing it as not met never ends anyone's employment by itself. Dismissal for misconduct follows the Labour Act's procedure (s.23–24: a show-cause notice with at least seven days to answer, and an inquiry), and other termination follows s.26, with notice or pay. Ask your legal adviser before acting on a plan.

## Probation reviews

Three weeks before a person's probation ends (Labour Act s.4), their manager is asked for a probation review, due a week before the end: ratings, comments and a recommendation to confirm, extend or end the probation. HR acts on it from the person's probation, as before. A recommendation never acts by itself.

## On the employee's page

The **Performance** tab on an employee's page shows HR their goals, reviews, improvement plans and probation reviews.
